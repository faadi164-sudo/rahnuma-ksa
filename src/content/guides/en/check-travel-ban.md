## The four kinds of travel ban

| Type | Who orders it | Typical cause | How it ends |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Enforcement (debt) | Enforcement court, at the creditor's request | Unpaid judgment, cheque or promissory note (loans, rent, car finance) | Payment or settlement, the court lifts it, or the term expires |
| Precautionary | The court hearing a case | Risk you leave before the case is decided | Court decision |
| Criminal | Criminal court or investigating authority | Criminal case or sentence | End of the set period or the authority's decision |
| Administrative | Ministry of Interior | Security reasons | End of the period or a ministry decision |

## Step by step: check on Absher

1. Log in to Absher Individuals.
2. Open the inquiries section under your services.
3. Choose the inquiry that shows travel restrictions or circulars (sometimes called the generalization report).
4. Read the result. It may not show the case number or the creditor.

Menu names change from time to time; use the search bar in Absher if you can't find it.

## Step by step: check Najiz (debts and enforcement)

1. Log in to Najiz with Nafath.
2. Choose **All electronic services**.
3. Open **Enforcement**.
4. Choose **Inquire about enforcement applications** and search.

## How a debt ban is lifted

- **Pay or settle.** Closing the enforcement request ends the ban. Keep proof of payment and make sure any private settlement is recorded in the file.
- **Ask the court.** It can lift a ban for reasons such as medical treatment abroad, a small debt, a job that requires travel, or assets that cover the debt.
- **Grievance.** If the order was a mistake, a grievance can be filed within 10 working days.

## What changed in late 2026

The new Enforcement Law came into force at the end of October 2026. Debt travel bans are now ordered only when the creditor asks, last up to 3 years per order (6 years in total), and end when the enforcement request ends.

## Common mistakes

- Checking only Absher. Debt cases show on Najiz.
- Booking flights before checking.
- Assuming an instalment agreement lifts the ban.
