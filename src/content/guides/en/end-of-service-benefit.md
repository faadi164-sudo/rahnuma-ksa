## The formula

1. **Years 1 to 5:** half a month's wage for each year.
2. **Each year after year 5:** one month's wage for each year.
3. **Part of a year:** paid in proportion to the time worked.
4. **Wage used:** your last wage, meaning basic pay plus regular allowances.

## If you resign

| Continuous service | Share of the award |
| --- | --- |
| Less than 2 years | None |
| 2 to 5 years | One third |
| More than 5 and less than 10 years | Two thirds |
| 10 years or more | Full award |

If your employer ends the contract (not for misconduct), or a fixed-term contract simply ends, the full award is due.

## Examples

Last wage of **SAR 6,000** a month (basic plus housing and transport).

| Situation | Calculation | Award (SAR) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 years, contract ended by employer | 4 × 3,000 | 12,000 |
| 4 years, resigned | 12,000 × 1/3 | 4,000 |
| 7 years, contract ended by employer | (5 × 3,000) + (2 × 6,000) | 27,000 |
| 7 years, resigned | 27,000 × 2/3 | 18,000 |
| 12 years, resigned | (5 × 3,000) + (7 × 6,000) | 57,000 |

> Timing matters. Resigning one month before your fifth anniversary can cost you a large share of the award. Check your exact start date on Qiwa before you hand in a resignation.

## Step by step: checking what you are owed

1. Find your contract start date and current wage on Qiwa.
2. Work out the full award with the formula above.
3. Apply the resignation share if you are resigning.
4. Compare it with your employer's final settlement.
5. If it is wrong or unpaid, raise it with HR first, then file a labor complaint.

## Common mistakes

- Calculating on basic salary only.
- Ignoring part years, which are paid in proportion.
- Resigning just before a 2-, 5- or 10-year threshold without checking the difference.
