When an employer ends a contract without a reason the law accepts, the worker is owed compensation under Article 77 of the Labor Law. This guide explains how much, when the employer owes nothing, when you can walk out and keep your rights, and how to claim.

## How much compensation

Unless your contract sets a different amount, Article 77 gives:

| Contract | Compensation |
| --- | --- |
| Fixed-term | Your wages for the rest of the contract |
| Open-ended (indefinite) | 15 days' wages for each year of service, pro rata for part years |
| Either | Never less than two months' wages |

Most expat contracts are fixed-term, so the usual measure is the wages for the time left on your contract. The wage used is generally your actual wage: your basic pay plus the regular allowances you receive, not basic pay alone.

## Worked examples

- **Fixed-term, 5 months left:** total wage SAR 6,000 a month. Compensation is 5 × 6,000 = **SAR 30,000**.
- **Fixed-term, 1 month left:** total wage SAR 6,000. One month's wages is below the minimum, so you get two months: **SAR 12,000**.
- **Open-ended, 3 years' service:** wage SAR 8,000. 15 days × 3 years = 45 days, or SAR 12,000. That is below the two-month minimum, so you get **SAR 16,000**.
- **Open-ended, 6 years' service:** wage SAR 8,000. 15 days × 6 = 90 days, or **SAR 24,000**.

On an open-ended contract, 15 days a year only passes the two-month minimum after four years of service.

## What you are owed on top

Article 77 compensation doesn't replace anything else. If you are dismissed without a valid reason, you are also owed:

- **Your end-of-service award:** half a month's wage for each of the first five years and a full month for each year after that. Use our [end-of-service calculator](/tools/end-of-service-calculator/).
- **Unused annual leave,** paid in cash.
- **Any unpaid salary** up to your last day.
- **Pay in lieu of notice,** where notice applies and wasn't given. This is a separate sum from Article 77 compensation.

## It works both ways

Compensation goes to whichever side is harmed. If you walk out of a fixed-term contract early without a legitimate reason, your employer can claim compensation from you on the same basis. Resign properly instead; see our [guide to resigning](/guides/resign-from-job-notice-period/).

## When the employer owes nothing: Article 80

An employer can end the contract without notice, end-of-service award or compensation only in nine cases, and only after giving you the chance to state your objection. In short:

1. Assaulting the employer, a manager or a colleague at work or because of work.
2. Failing your core duties, disobeying lawful orders, or deliberately ignoring posted safety rules after a written warning.
3. Proven misconduct or dishonesty.
4. Deliberately causing material loss, reported to the authorities within 24 hours.
5. Getting the job by forgery.
6. Being on probation.
7. Unexcused absence of more than 30 days in a contract year or more than 15 days in a row, after a written warning at 20 or 10 days.
8. Using your position for personal gain.
9. Disclosing the employer's industrial or commercial secrets.

If your dismissal doesn't fit one of these, the employer can't use Article 80 to avoid paying.

## When you can leave and keep your rights: Article 81

You can leave without notice and keep all your statutory rights, including your end-of-service award, if:

1. The employer fails its essential contract or legal duties to you, such as paying your wages.
2. You were deceived about the job terms when hired.
3. You are given substantially different work without your consent.
4. You or your family face violence or immoral conduct from the employer, a family member or a manager.
5. You are treated cruelly, unjustly or insultingly.
6. A serious danger to your health or safety is known to the employer and not removed.
7. The employer's conduct forces you to leave, so that it looks as if you ended the contract.

Keep evidence: messages, payslips, Mudad salary records and witness names.

## Protected from dismissal

A woman may not be dismissed while pregnant, on maternity leave, or ill because of either.

## How to claim, step by step

1. **Get it in writing.** Ask for a written termination letter with the date and reason, and check your contract status on Qiwa.
2. **Don't sign away your rights.** Read any settlement or clearance carefully before signing that you have received everything.
3. **Work out what you are owed:** compensation, end-of-service, unused leave and unpaid wages.
4. **File with friendly settlement.** The Ministry's free friendly settlement service is mandatory before court, usually with two sessions within 21 working days. If it fails, the case goes to the labor court through Najiz. See our [guide to labor complaints](/guides/labor-complaint-friendly-settlement/).
5. **Act within 12 months** of your last day. Later claims are generally not accepted.

## Your iqama after a dismissal

When your contract ends, your status shows as disconnected from work for 60 days. In that time you need to transfer to a new employer, take a final exit, or sign a new contract. If you do none of these, you can be recorded as absent from work, which makes everything harder. See our guides to [changing jobs](/guides/job-transfer-without-employer-consent/) and the [final exit checklist](/guides/final-exit-checklist/).
