## How the system tracks late salaries

| Day after the due date | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Mudad asks the employer to upload wage data |
| 10 | Second notice to the employer |
| 15 | Final warning |
| 20 | Mudad asks the Ministry for an inspection visit |

## If your employer gives an excuse

1. The employer has 10 days to justify the delay in the system.
2. You then have **3 days** to accept or reject it. If you don't answer, the employer's excuse is processed automatically.
3. Reject it if it isn't true, and keep proof (bank statements, messages).

## If the salary is still unpaid

- **30 days after the due date:** you can file an electronic enforcement request on **Najiz** (Ministry of Justice). Your contract must be documented in Qiwa.
- **2 months:** most Ministry services are suspended for the employer, except work permit renewal and issuance.
- **3 months:** all Ministry services are suspended, and you can **transfer to a new employer without your current employer's consent**, even if your work permit is still valid.

## Step by step: protect yourself

1. Check that your contract is documented in Qiwa.
2. Keep salary slips, bank statements and any messages about the delay.
3. Respond to any justification in the system within 3 days.
4. If the delay continues, file through the Ministry's friendly settlement service, or an enforcement request on Najiz after 30 days.
5. After 3 months unpaid, look for a new employer; it can request your transfer in Qiwa without the old employer's approval.

## Common mistakes

- Ignoring the 3-day window to reject the employer's excuse.
- Walking out without documenting anything.
- Accepting a "settlement" in cash with no written record.
