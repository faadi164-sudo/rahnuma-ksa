## The standard route: job mobility through Qiwa

You can move without your current employer's consent if **all** of these apply:

1. You are an expatriate worker covered by the Saudi Labor Law.
2. You have completed one year with your current employer since you first entered the Kingdom.
3. You have a documented work contract.
4. Your new employer submits a job offer through Qiwa.
5. Your current employer is notified of the transfer, with a notice period.

The new employer must also be eligible: allowed to obtain work visas and compliant with the Wage Protection Program, contract documentation on Qiwa and the self-evaluation program.

## Eight cases that allow a transfer without consent

1. Your contract was not documented within three months of your arrival.
2. Your wages have not been paid for three consecutive months.
3. Your employer is absent because of travel, imprisonment, death or similar.
4. Your work permit or iqama has expired.
5. You report your employer's involvement in commercial concealment (tasattur), and you are not involved yourself.
6. You have proof your employer is involved in human trafficking.
7. In a labor dispute, your employer missed two court or amicable settlement sessions after being notified.
8. Your current employer agrees to the transfer.

## Step by step

1. Check your contract and its start date on Qiwa.
2. Your new employer sends you a job offer through Qiwa.
3. Accept the offer in your Qiwa account.
4. Qiwa notifies your current employer and the notice period begins.
5. After the notice period, the transfer completes and your iqama moves to the new employer.

## Common mistakes

- Resigning before the new offer is on Qiwa. Get the offer first.
- Assuming a transfer is impossible in year one. The eight cases still apply.
- Not checking whether your contract is documented on Qiwa; it affects which rules apply.
