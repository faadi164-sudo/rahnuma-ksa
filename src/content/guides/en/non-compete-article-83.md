> This guide explains the law in general. It isn't legal advice. For a real dispute, speak to a lawyer or the Ministry of Human Resources.

## The conditions for a valid non-compete

All of these must be met together:

| Condition | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Access to clients or secrets** | Your job let you know the employer's clients or its business secrets |
| **In writing** | The clause is written in the contract or a signed agreement |
| **Limited in time** | No more than **two years** after the job ends |
| **Limited in place** | A defined area, not "anywhere" |
| **Limited in type of work** | Only work that actually competes with the employer |

If any one of these is missing, the clause lacks a condition of validity.

## Non-compete vs confidentiality

- **Non-compete:** stops you working for a competitor or competing yourself. Two-year maximum.
- **Confidentiality:** stops you revealing the employer's secrets. It must also be written and defined, but the two-year limit in the law applies to the non-compete, not to confidentiality.

You can join a competitor in a way that respects confidentiality, as long as you don't take or use the old employer's confidential information.

## Before you accept a job with a competitor

1. **Find the clause.** Check your signed contract and the contract on Qiwa.
2. **Test it against the five conditions above.** Note the duration, area and type of work it names.
3. **Check your role.** Did you actually deal with clients or have access to business secrets?
4. **Don't take anything.** No client lists, files, price lists or emails.
5. **Get advice** if the clause looks valid and the new job is in the same field and area.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming every non-compete is unenforceable. A well-drafted one can be valid.
- Assuming every non-compete is valid. Many are too broad.
- Taking client data to the new job, which can breach confidentiality whatever the non-compete says.
