## Step by step: the full list (with a registered number)

1. Open the CST Mutasil individuals service.
2. Switch the language to English.
3. Enter your iqama number and date of birth.
4. When asked if you have a mobile number, choose **Yes** and enter one of your numbers.
5. Click **Inquire** and enter the code sent by SMS.
6. You will see each SIM: the network, number and type of subscription.

## Step by step: a quick count (no registered number)

1. Follow steps 1 to 3 above.
2. Choose **No** when asked about a mobile number.
3. Click **Inquire**. You will see how many SIMs each network has registered under your iqama, but not the numbers.

## If you find a number you don't recognise

1. Note the network and number.
2. Contact the network's customer service and ask them to cancel it, or file a complaint through the CST website.
3. Check again after a few days to confirm it has gone.

## Why every SIM is tied to you

Since January 2016, everyone buying a SIM card in Saudi Arabia has been fingerprinted, and the data is shared with the National Information Center to confirm who the buyer is. Existing SIMs that weren't verified in the grace period were suspended. This is why a SIM bought under your iqama stays your responsibility even if someone else is using it.

If you can't give fingerprints, CST's SIM authentication code service lets you verify with a temporary code approved in Nafath. The same code works to issue a new or additional SIM, replace a lost SIM, move your number to another network, or activate a SIM for the person who actually uses it.

## SIMs your family or workers use

A common situation: you bought a SIM in your name for your wife, a son or a domestic worker before they had their own iqama. It works for calls, but it causes problems later:

- **Absher and Nafath** need a mobile number registered on the user's own iqama. Their account can't be activated or recovered with a number on yours.
- **Banks** also need a number in the account holder's own name to open an account online.
- **Fines and misuse** on that line come back to you.

Once they have an iqama, get them a SIM in their own name, update their Absher number, then cancel the line on your iqama. See our guide to changing the mobile number in Absher.

## Before a final exit

A final exit visa needs all your dues cleared, and that includes phone bills. Before you apply:

1. Run the Mutasil check above to see every line on your iqama.
2. Pay any postpaid balances, then cancel those lines with each network.
3. Cancel prepaid lines you won't use rather than leaving them active under your iqama.
4. Keep the number registered in Absher working until you have left, because verification codes go to it.

## Good habits

- Check before your final exit, and after giving your iqama copy to any shop or agent.
- Cancel old lines you no longer use instead of letting them lapse with unpaid bills.
- Never lend your iqama copy for someone else's SIM, and never use a SIM registered to another person.
